👁️ What is eye tracking?

Eye tracking measures where you look, for how long, and how your eyes move. The result is a stream of gaze points: coordinates on the screen, captured anywhere from 60 to 1,000+ times a second. A game or app can use them to know what you’re paying attention to before you click anything.

Why is that so useful? Because you only see sharply in a tiny spot. Move your cursor over the fuzzy text below to see your “fovea” at work.

Right now, only a patch about the size of your thumbnail at arm’s length is razor sharp. Your brain builds a detailed picture of the world by jumping your eyes around three or four times every second, and an eye tracker records every one of those jumps.

Fixations

Your eyes pause for roughly 200 to 300 milliseconds. This is when you actually take in information, so trackers count and time them.

⚡

Saccades

The super-fast jumps between fixations, among the quickest movements your body makes. Vision is mostly suppressed during them.

🔍

Pupil size

Pupils change with light, and also with mental effort and excitement, so researchers read them like a tiny mood meter.

🧠 How it works

Most trackers follow the same recipe: light up the eye, take pictures, find two landmarks, then do some clever geometry.

Watch the dark pupil slide around while the bright glint stays put. The gap between them is the clue.

💡

1. Light the eye

Invisible near-infrared LEDs shine on your face. It doesn’t bother you, and it makes the pupil stand out clearly in any room.

📷

2. Take pictures

A fast infrared camera grabs dozens or hundreds of close-up eye images every second.

⚫

3. Find the landmarks

Software spots the pupil centre and the tiny glint of the LEDs on your cornea. The pupil turns with your eye, the glint barely moves.

🎯

4. Calibrate

You stare at 5 to 9 dots. The system learns how your pupil-to-glint distance maps to spots on the screen, because every eye and face is different.

🧭

5. Estimate and smooth

Every frame, the learned mapping turns the eye image into a screen point. Filters remove jitter and label each moment as a fixation or a saccade.

Webcam-only trackers skip the infrared and use machine learning on regular video. They’re cheaper but less precise. Dedicated trackers can tell where you look to within about a degree or better.

From gaze to game

Eyes can’t click, so eye-controlled games use dwell: look at something long enough and it activates. That’s exactly how Cat Blaster’s lock-on ring works. It’s also why the ring fills slowly: it stops you from firing at everything your eyes sweep over.
